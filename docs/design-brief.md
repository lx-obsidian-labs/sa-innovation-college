## 1. Business Goals

### What the College Is Trying to Achieve

SA Innovation College is a private education institution in Centurion, Gauteng, operating in a crowded market alongside public TVET colleges, private higher education brands (Richfield, Emeris, Rosebank College), and online learning platforms. The college needs to:

- **Establish credibility.** The current website is a generic digital agency template ("Beyond") with SA Innovation content loosely layered on top. CTA buttons link to Elementor library pages, social media links are broken, and the About page literally reads "Behind the scenes at Beyond." The website currently undermines trust rather than building it.
- **Drive enrolment.** Every page, every element, every interaction must move prospective students toward applying or enquiring. There is no room for vanity content that does not serve conversion.
- **Differentiate from TVET colleges.** Public TVET colleges in the same region (Tshwane South, Ekurhuleni West) offer similar vocational programmes. SA Innovation must communicate what makes private education a better choice: smaller classes, career support, industry partnerships, flexibility, or outcomes.
- **Prove outcomes.** The site claims 5,000+ students, 15+ programmes, and a 95% success rate. These are currently unverified. Either substantiate them with real data or remove them. Vague claims destroy credibility faster than no claims at all.

### Primary Conversion Goals

| Priority | Goal | Measurement |
|----------|------|-------------|
| 1 | Programme enquiry submission | Form completions per month |
| 2 | Application initiation | "Apply Now" clicks per month |
| 3 | WhatsApp contact | WhatsApp click-through rate |
| 4 | Phone call | Click-to-call taps per month |

### Secondary Goals

| Priority | Goal | Measurement |
|----------|------|-------------|
| 5 | Prospectus download | Downloads per month (lead capture) |
| 6 | Programme page engagement | Time on programme detail pages |
| 7 | Return visits | Returning visitor rate |
| 8 | Partner/accreditation trust | Scroll depth past trust indicators |

### Business Metrics That Matter

- **Enquiry-to-application conversion rate.** How many people who enquire actually apply?
- **Programme page to enquiry rate.** Are programme pages persuading visitors to act?
- **Mobile conversion rate.** The majority of SA users are on mobile. If mobile converts poorly, the site is failing.
- **Cost per enquiry.** If running ads, how much does each enquiry cost?
- **Time to first contact.** How quickly after landing on the site does a visitor reach out?

## 2. User Goals (Student Journeys)

### What Type of Institution Is This?

SA Innovation College is a private TVET-level and short-course provider. It is not a university. It is not a public TVET college. It occupies the space between: accredited private education that is more accessible than university, more structured than informal training, and more career-focused than public TVET.

### What Qualifications Does It Offer?

Based on the live site (all details are unverified until confirmed with the client):

| Programme | Type | Status |
|-----------|------|--------|
| Computer Literacy | Short Course (FREE) | Confirmed on site |
| Table Service Course | Short Course | Confirmed on site |
| Traffic Officer Course (National Certificate in Tactical Road Traffic Operations) | Qualification | Confirmed on site |
| Hospitality and Tourism | Programme Area (details unknown) | Confirmed on site |

**Critical gap:** The current site lists zero detailed programme information. No fees, no durations, no entry requirements, no NQF levels, no intake dates. This must be resolved before launch.

### Who Is the Target Student?

| Dimension | Profile |
|-----------|---------|
| Age | 18-35, skewing younger (18-25 core) |
| Location | Gauteng, particularly Centurion, Pretoria, and surrounding areas |
| Education level | Matric or equivalent; some may have incomplete matric |
| Motivation | Get a job, get a better job, gain a practical skill, change careers |
| Constraints | Data costs, device limitations (mobile-primary), time (many work while studying), financial pressure |
| Decision drivers | Affordability, accreditation, employment outcomes, flexibility, proximity |
| Information needs | What will I study? How long? What does it cost? Is it accredited? Will I get a job? |

### What Is the Primary Conversion Goal?

**Enquiry first, application second.** Most prospective students at this level are not ready to apply on first visit. They need to ask questions: "Am I eligible?" "Can I afford this?" "When does the next intake start?" The site must make enquiring feel low-risk and fast.

### What Differentiates This College from TVET Colleges and Universities?

This is the single most important question the website must answer. Based on the available data, the likely differentiators are:

- **Accessibility.** Lower entry requirements than universities; more structured than self-study.
- **Speed.** Short courses can be completed in weeks, not years.
- **Industry partnerships.** Training and corporate partners suggest employer-connected outcomes.
- **Location.** Centurion-based, convenient for southern Pretoria residents.
- **Free offerings.** The Computer Literacy course is free, which is a powerful entry point.

**These differentiators are assumptions.** The client must confirm what actually sets them apart.

### What Problems Do Prospective Students Have?

1. **Confusion about options.** "There are so many colleges. Which one is legitimate? Which one will actually help me get a job?"
2. **Fear of wasting money.** "What if I pay fees and get a qualification nobody recognises?"
3. **Uncertainty about outcomes.** "Will I actually be employable after this?"
4. **Logistical anxiety.** "Can I afford the fees? Can I get there? Does it fit around my work?"
5. **Distrust of marketing.** Every college claims to be the best. Students have learned to discount marketing claims.

### Why Would Someone Choose This College Over Alternatives?

The website must make this answerable in 10 seconds or less. The answer should be a combination of:

- Accredited programmes with recognised qualifications
- Employer-connected training through corporate partnerships
- Affordable fees (or free entry-point courses)
- Centurion location with accessible campus
- Proven graduate outcomes (if verifiable)

### What Information Do Students Need Before Applying?

| Stage | Information Needed |
|-------|--------------------|
| Awareness | What programmes exist? What area are they in? |
| Interest | What does each programme involve? Duration? Cost? Entry requirements? |
| Consideration | Is it accredited? What do graduates say? What are the career outcomes? |
| Decision | How do I apply? When does the next intake start? What financial aid is available? |
| Action | Application form, contact details, campus visit booking |

## 3. Website Goals

### What Must the Website Accomplish?

1. **Replace the current broken site with a credible, professional presence.** The existing site actively damages the college's reputation. This is the baseline.
2. **Make programme information findable in under 3 clicks.** A visitor should be able to go from homepage to a specific programme's details (fees, duration, requirements) within 3 taps or clicks.
3. **Convert visitors into enquiries.** Every page must have a clear, low-friction path to contact.
4. **Work flawlessly on mobile.** Not "responsive" as an afterthought. Mobile-first, designed for thumb navigation, fast on 3G, data-conscious.
5. **Establish trust through design, content, and social proof.** Accreditation badges, real testimonials, partner logos, and transparent information must be visible without hunting.

### What Must It Feel Like?

- **Trustworthy.** A parent should feel comfortable recommending this college to their child.
- **Accessible.** A first-generation university applicant should not feel intimidated or confused.
- **Professional but not corporate.** This is not a bank. It is not a law firm. It is a place where real people learn real skills.
- **South African.** The site should feel rooted in its context. Not a copy of an American online learning platform. Not a generic template. Locally relevant.
- **Fast.** Every page should load in under 3 seconds on a 3G connection. Speed is a trust signal.

### What Must It NOT Feel Like?

- **A template.** The current site feels like a WordPress theme with content pasted in. The new site must feel bespoke and intentional.
- **A sales page.** Aggressive "BUY NOW" energy will alienate this audience. The tone should be inviting, not pushy.
- **An information dump.** Walls of text about accreditation bodies and institutional history will lose visitors. Content must be scannable and prioritised.
- **A tech startup.** Over-designed animations, parallax effects, and cutting-edge UI patterns will feel alien to the target audience. Simplicity wins.
- **A government site.** Cold, bureaucratic, link-heavy pages with no visual hierarchy. This is not a licensing portal.

## 4. Brand Personality Direction

### Modern vs. Traditional

**Land at: 65% Modern / 35% Traditional**

The site should feel contemporary and clean, but not trendy to the point of alienating a conservative audience. Use modern layout patterns (card-based programme discovery, sticky CTAs, mobile-first navigation) but ground them with traditional trust signals (accreditation badges, physical address, phone number, professional photography). Avoid design trends that will age quickly (glassmorphism, heavy gradients, brutalist typography).

### Premium vs. Affordable

**Land at: 60% Premium / 40% Affordable**

The site should communicate quality without communicating exclusivity. The design must say "this is a professional institution" not "this is cheap." But it must also signal accessibility. The free Computer Literacy course and affordable short courses are entry points -- the site should not make low-income students feel unwelcome. Use clean white space, quality photography, and restrained colour usage to signal premium. Avoid cluttered layouts and overly busy designs that signal budget.

### Professional vs. Casual

**Land at: 70% Professional / 30% Casual**

This is an educational institution. The tone must be authoritative and credible. But it should not feel cold or bureaucratic. Use warm language, real student stories, and human photography to add approachability. Headlines should be direct and confident, not corporate jargon. "Learn the skills employers actually need" is better than "Leveraging synergistic competencies for optimal workforce readiness."

### Technical vs. Human

**Land at: 40% Technical / 60% Human**

Lead with people, not programmes. Show students, graduates, classrooms, and outcomes. Use technical details (NQF levels, accreditation bodies, module lists) as supporting information, not the primary narrative. The homepage should feel like a conversation with a knowledgeable advisor, not a course catalogue.

### Aspirational vs. Practical

**Land at: 50% Aspirational / 50% Practical**

The tagline "Knowledge Growth Excellence" is aspirational. The course offerings are practical. Both are valid. Use aspirational language in hero sections and CTAs ("Your future starts here," "Grow towards excellence"). Use practical language in programme details (fees, duration, requirements, outcomes). The homepage hero should inspire; the programme page should inform.

### Confident vs. Modest

**Land at: 65% Confident / 35% Modest**

The site should project confidence without arrogance. State facts directly: "We have trained over 5,000 students" (if verified). "Our graduates work in road traffic management across Gauteng." Avoid false modesty and weasel words ("we try our best," "we aim to"). Also avoid inflated superlatives ("the best college in Africa," "world-class education"). Confidence comes from evidence, not from shouting.

### Academic vs. Vocational

**Land at: 30% Academic / 70% Vocational**

This is not a university. The site should not feel academic. It should feel career-oriented and practical. Use language like "get job-ready," "learn skills you can use," "gain qualifications employers recognise." Reference specific job outcomes where possible: "Graduates of the Traffic Officer programme work for municipal and provincial traffic departments." NQF levels and accreditation matter, but frame them as proof of value, not as academic credentials.

### Corporate vs. Friendly

**Land at: 50% Corporate / 50% Friendly**

Balance institutional credibility with personal warmth. The header and footer should feel corporate (logo, navigation, accreditation, legal). The body content should feel friendly (student photos, testimonials, FAQ sections, WhatsApp contact). The floating WhatsApp button is the right instinct -- it says "we are approachable." The site should feel like a college that a real person runs, not a faceless institution.

## 5. Key Design Principles

### 1. Mobile First, Data Conscious

Every design decision must be evaluated against: "Does this work on a 5-inch screen over a 3G connection?" Lazy-load images, avoid auto-playing video, keep page weight minimal. Design for the worst-case scenario and enhance for better conditions. This is not optional in the South African market.

### 2. Clarity Over Cleverness

If a visitor has to think about where to click, what a section means, or how to find programme information, the design has failed. Navigation labels should be literal ("Programmes" not "Discover"). CTAs should say exactly what they do ("Apply Now" not "Get Started"). Page structure should follow a predictable hierarchy. Confusion is the enemy of conversion.

### 3. Trust Is Shown, Not Claimed

Do not say "we are trustworthy." Show accreditation badges. Show partner logos. Show real student testimonials with names and photos. Show the physical address. Show the phone number. Show transparent fee information. Every trust signal must be verifiable. If a claim cannot be verified, remove it.

### 4. Every Page Has One Job

Each page must have a single, clear purpose. The homepage exists to route visitors to programme information and convert them into enquiries. The programme listing page exists to help visitors find the right programme. The programme detail page exists to convince them to apply. The contact page exists to capture enquiries. If a page tries to do everything, it does nothing well.

### 5. Content Before Container

Design the content first, then build the layout to serve it. Do not create beautiful empty containers that need to be filled. Start with the actual programme information, the real testimonials, the verified statistics. If the content does not exist yet, design placeholders that make the gap obvious and motivate content creation.

### 6. Progressive Disclosure

Show the minimum information needed at each stage. Homepage shows programme categories, not every module. Programme listing shows cards with key metadata, not full descriptions. Programme detail page reveals fees, requirements, and modules on demand. FAQ sections use accordions. This reduces cognitive overload and keeps pages scannable.

### 7. Accessibility Is Non-Negotiable

WCAG 2.2 AA compliance minimum. This means: sufficient colour contrast (4.5:1 for body text), keyboard navigability, screen reader compatibility, focus states on all interactive elements, alt text on all images, and minimum 48px touch targets. An education institution that excludes people with disabilities has no business claiming "excellence."

## 6. Success Criteria

### Quantitative Metrics (Measure 90 Days Post-Launch)

| Metric | Current Baseline | Target | How to Measure |
|--------|-------------------|--------|----------------|
| Mobile page load time | Unknown (likely > 5s on Elementor) | < 3s on 3G | Lighthouse, WebPageTest |
| Enquiry form submissions | 0 trackable (broken forms) | 30+ per month | Form analytics |
| WhatsApp click-through | 0 trackable (broken link) | 50+ per month | UTM tracking |
| Programme page bounce rate | Unknown | < 45% | Google Analytics |
| Mobile conversion rate | Unknown | > 2% | Google Analytics goals |
| Lighthouse Performance score | Unknown | > 90 | Lighthouse |
| Lighthouse Accessibility score | Unknown (96 images missing alt text) | > 95 | Lighthouse |

### Qualitative Success Indicators

- **Client can update content without a developer.** The CMS or content management approach must be usable by non-technical staff.
- **Every CTA button links to a real page.** Zero broken internal links. Zero links to third-party template libraries.
- **All social media links work.** Currently all four are broken. Post-launch, every social link must point to a real profile or be removed entirely.
- **Contact information is correct.** The current contact page has placeholder data (wrong phone, wrong email, wrong address). The new site must display verified information on every page.
- **Accreditation information is visible.** On the homepage and in the footer, with links to verification where possible.
- **The site does not look like a template.** A visitor should not be able to identify the underlying framework by looking at the design. The site should feel custom-built for SA Innovation College.
- **Prospective students can answer three questions within 10 seconds of landing on the homepage:** What does this college offer? Is it legitimate? How do I apply?
